Construct a map, establish preservation, and transport closure

Inputs and types: The shared library supplies Foundation.Group, Theory.Hom,
and Foundation.prodGroup. A raw function becomes a Hom only after providing its
toFun and map_mul fields. Theory.MulClosed g P means that P is preserved by
the multiplication g.mul.

Premises: Obtain multiplication preservation for the intended map and closure
of the chosen target predicate. Neither follows merely from a function type or
from an arbitrary predicate. Closure under multiplication alone does not assert
identity membership or inverse closure.

Choices: Express the desired constraints as a predicate on the image of a map.
Select the codomain and target predicate together. A composite map may use
successive preservation equations; multiple observations may use a product
codomain. The diagonal predicate on a product expresses equality of coordinates.

Steps: Construct the Hom locally. Prove its map_mul field using the supplied
equations, equality transport, and coordinate equalities as appropriate. Prove
target-predicate closure locally. Apply Theory.preimage_mul_closed to that Hom,
predicate, and closure proof. If an existential output requests a map, return
the constructed object together with its pointwise agreement and closure evidence.

Outputs and residual obligations: The result is multiplication closure of the
preimage predicate, with an explicit map and checked preservation. Keep raw
functions, bundled maps, product projections, and their types aligned. Agreement
with a required raw function must be shown, not inferred from a matching name.

Boundaries: Failure to construct the needed map or prove the target predicate
closed means this transport rule has unmet premises. It does not prove the goal
impossible. When asked for a counterexample, give actual witnesses and check the
failed implication. Any permitted proof may be used; this guide is not a claim
of novelty, automatic method discovery, or general transfer.
